What works

The hierarchy survives every renderer, including terminals and apps with limited styling APIs. Near-white surfaces stay legible without looking clinical.

What risks

It can feel conservative if iconography, motion, and the Mekanikos blue do not provide the small note of character.

Pick this if

You want the strongest default and the least drift across Linux, macOS, editors, browser CSS, and documentation.

What works

The shell feels integrated with the desktop rather than pasted over it. Layered edges keep translucent cards crisp.

What risks

Wallpaper contrast changes constantly, and blur or alpha semantics do not translate cleanly to every app.

Pick this if

The shell itself is the flagship surface and native implementations may use simpler solid-color fallbacks elsewhere.

What works

The name Mekanikos becomes visible in the system: measured, exact, and tool-like. Tokens are especially easy to inspect and document.

What risks

Mono-led typography and harder geometry can become tiring when applied indiscriminately.

Pick this if

You want a stronger identity and are willing to reserve humanist type for long-form content.

Optimizes for calm, friendly surfaces with almost no chrome · Tradeoff boundaries rely more heavily on careful tonal contrast.

What works

It removes ornamental lines and lets spacing carry the hierarchy. The warmer accent gives the system a quieter personality.

What risks

Closely related surface tones can collapse under dim displays, transparency, or limited-color integrations.

Pick this if

You prefer a friendlier desktop and accept explicit border fallbacks for accessibility and low-fidelity targets.

How to choose

Pick the governing grammar first; individual tokens can be tuned after the structure is agreed.
